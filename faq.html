Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions parents ask most.

Should my child prepare at all?

ACER says short-term training is unlikely to help and that wide reading and critical thinking are the best preparation. It still provides practice tests to build familiarity. Steady practice that focuses on understanding mistakes is a sensible middle path.

How long should we prepare?

Sources suggest anything from about 8 weeks to a year or more, and none gives evidence. Start early, practise steadily and review mistakes.

Can anyone guarantee a scholarship?

No. Outcomes depend on how strong the student is to start with, effort, test conditions and how competitive each school is. Be wary of anyone who promises a place.

Do I have to sit the test more than once?

Not if all your schools are in the same cooperative sitting. Schools on different dates each use their own test, so your child sits each one.

Do wrong answers lose marks?

ACER says no for its scholarship tests. Check the rules of your own test.

Is the Victorian selective exam the same as a scholarship test?

No. It is a state-wide ACER exam for Year 9 entry to four government schools, with its own format. Scholarships are run school by school.

How long should I practise each day?

Short, regular sessions beat long cramming. There is no official figure, and anyone quoting a precise number of hours is guessing.

Can ScholarPrep mark writing?

No. It gives prompts, a planning step and a checklist. A teacher or tutor needs to read the piece.

Are the questions from real papers?

No. They are original practice questions modelled on common formats. ScholarPrep is not affiliated with ACER or Edutest.
