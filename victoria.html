Victoria: selective schools and scholarships

The four selective high schools, the Year 8 exam, and how scholarships are a separate path.

The four selective high schools

Victoria has four fully selective government high schools: Melbourne High School, Mac.Robertson Girls' High School, Nossal High School and Suzanne Cory High School. Entry is in Year 9 through one state-wide exam run by ACER and sat in Year 8, usually around the middle of the year. Apply through the official selective entry process and check the dates for your year.

What the exam looks like

The whole session runs about four hours including registration and breaks, with 2 hours 35 minutes of actual testing:

PartTime
Mathematics and Quantitative Reasoning60 minutes
Break20 minutes
Reading and Verbal Reasoning55 minutes
Short break5 minutes
Writing (two tasks)40 minutes

Answers to the reasoning tests are shaded on an answer booklet that is machine scanned, so follow the on-the-day instructions exactly. No section needs knowledge beyond the Year 8 curriculum. The exam is built to challenge: it is not unusual to get only about half the questions right, and many students do not finish.

Older five-option papers

Older practice papers for this exam look different. They use five options (A to E, often with “None of these”), give 30 minutes per section, and cover Reading Comprehension (50 questions), Mathematics (60), Numerical Reasoning (50) and Verbal Reasoning (60), plus a creative and a persuasive writing task, each with 5 minutes to plan and 30 to write. Students were told they do not lose marks for wrong answers. Their maths went beyond Year 8 (surds, indices, factorising, trigonometry), whereas the current exam is described as needing only Year 8 content. Newer papers have four options and different section names, so check which format your year uses. ScholarEdge has an exam option for each.

Practise with official samples too

The test provider publishes free sample questions for this exam. Do those as well, because they show the real layout, and they are not necessarily as hard as the live exam. ScholarEdge's questions are original and aim at the same kinds of thinking: multi-step maths with ratios, algebra and geometry, number and rearrangement patterns, analogies, odd-one-out and logical deduction, and reading questions about quotations, loaded wording and unstated assumptions.

Scholarships are a separate path

Independent and some Catholic schools run their own academic scholarships, usually using an ACER or Edutest test, and often sat the year before entry. Each school sets its own year levels and dates, so make a shortlist and check each school's scholarship page. The same core skills (reasoning, comprehension, careful timing) serve both paths.

Other options

Some government schools also run SEAL programs (Select Entry Accelerated Learning) with their own entry tests. Check each school.

The selective entry exam and the independent-school scholarship tests are different exams with different formats. Make sure you are preparing for the one your child is actually sitting.
