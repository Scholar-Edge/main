How scholarship tests work

Who sets the tests, how sittings work, and what is really on the paper. Where sources disagree, we say so.

Rewritten in our own words from ACER's published information and several preparation providers. Dates and rules change every year, so confirm them with ACER and each school.

Who writes the tests

Most independent schools buy in their scholarship test rather than writing one. ACER is the best known provider. Edutest and Academic Assessment Services are the other two commonly named, according to the tutoring company Exam Success. Which test your child sits depends on the school, so check each school's scholarship page.

Cooperative and alternative sittings

Cooperative: many schools test on one Saturday in February chosen by ACER. You can apply to up to six schools, sit once at your first preference, and the result goes to every school you applied to. Alternative date: a school picks its own date, your child sits at that school, and only that school sees the result. ACER notes that most Victorian schools use alternative dates.

When checked on 13 October 2026, ACER's parent page listed the cooperative sitting for 2028 entry as Saturday 27 February 2027 (Qld, NSW, NT, WA and Vic schools), with applications open from 21 September 2026 to 7 February 2027. SA schools in the ACEP program test on 6 February 2027, with applications closing 22 January 2027. Confirm these before relying on them.

What is on the test

Primary tests cover reading and viewing, mathematics and writing. Secondary tests cover written expression, humanities (reading and interpreting a range of texts) and mathematics. Exam Success describes a Level 1 paper as two writing tasks plus a humanities test and a maths test, and estimates about 25 minutes per writing task and about 40 minutes each for roughly 45 humanities and 40 maths multiple-choice questions. Those numbers are their estimates, not ACER's, and schools can customise the paper. As a planning figure, expect around a minute per multiple-choice question.

How hard is it

ACER says the tests aim to find the highest-ability candidates within an already high-ability group, and that they are meant to be challenging. They test thinking (interpreting, inferring, applying ideas to new situations) more than recall of school content. Maths questions often use simple arithmetic in unfamiliar ways.

Edutest

Two commercial providers (Selectivetrial and Learnmate) describe Edutest papers as five parts: reading, mathematics, numerical reasoning, verbal reasoning and writing. We did not find Edutest's own format page, so treat this as a guide and check with Edutest and your school.

What ACER's own example paper says

ACER's 2017 Scholarship Tests: Example Questions sheet describes itself as examples of question formats for every test level. For Written Expression it says two topics are set and 25 minutes are allowed to plan, write and edit, that you may write a story, letter, discussion or argument, and that marking looks at what you have to say, how you organise it and how you express yourself. Short, lively and interesting is the aim, and the sample task uses a picture as the starting point. Mathematics questions are multiple choice, need no difficult calculation, and are done without a calculator. They ask you to use what you know to solve new problems. Humanities uses stimulus material of several kinds (informative writing, poems, stories, diagrams, cartoons or drawings), grouped into units with several questions each, and everything you need is in the material itself.

The sheet shows formats, not difficulty. Use it to learn the layout, then practise with harder material than the examples.

Where the sources disagree

Several preparation sites describe the ACER scholarship test differently from ACER and from each other:

  • Sections. ACER's own tips sheet and two prep providers describe maths, humanities and written expression. Learnmate lists four sections (reading comprehension, verbal reasoning, abstract reasoning, written expression), which we could not match to any ACER page.
  • Writing. One site says two 25-minute tasks. Another says you choose one of two prompts in 25 minutes in total. ACER's 2017 example sheet says two topics are set and 25 minutes are allowed, which points to 25 minutes overall, but check your test's current instructions.
  • Question counts. Estimates run from about 35 to 45 questions in 40 minutes.
  • Food. ACER says a snack for the break is fine. One guide says not to bring food. Follow ACER and your school.
  • Results. One guide says results are percentile ranks and schools set percentile cut-offs. ACER's parent page only says how results reach schools and parents, so ask how your school reports.

When in doubt, go with ACER's own pages and your school's instructions.
